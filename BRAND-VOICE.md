# FD Grill brand voice

How FD Grill sounds, on the website, menus, social posts, emails and texts.

**Positioning:** Home of the African grill.
**Tagline:** From our family's table to yours.

Use them together: the positioning says what we are, the tagline says how it feels. "Home of the African grill. From our family's table to yours."

## Who we are, as a person

If FD Grill were a person, it would be the aunt or uncle running the grill at a family gathering. They've cooked these dishes for more than 20 years, they know every one by name, and they'd rather hand you a plate than give a speech. Proud of the food, generous with guests, calm under a crowd.

## Who we're talking to

**Primary: food lovers who chase fire-cooked food.** They follow pitmasters and grill cooks, care where ingredients come from, and want to know *how* something was cooked, not just what it is. They're curious about West African food, and they'll try maafe or Djouka if we tell them what makes it good.

**Secondary: hosts planning an event.** Families, offices and churches who want food their guests will talk about, and someone reliable to cook it.

What both want from us: specifics. The fire, the spices, the cut, the hours on the stove. We write for them as fellow food people, never as "foodies" (we don't call them that in copy).

## Voice attributes

### Fire-forward
- **We are:** sensory and precise about how food is cooked: live fire, charcoal, smoke, char, the crackle of fat on coals, stews that simmer low and slow.
- **We are not:** gimmicky. No "flavor explosions", "mouthwatering", "to die for". And we never claim a technique we don't use: only say "wood-smoked" or "wood-fired" when the food is cooked over wood.
- **Sounds like:** "Kabobs marinated in house spices, then charred over live charcoal until the edges crisp."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Our mouthwatering BBQ will blow your mind!"

### Warm, like family
- **We are:** welcoming, personal, glad you're here. We talk about "your people", "your table", "your day".
- **We are not:** gushing, cute, or over-familiar. No "Hey foodies!"
- **Sounds like:** "Feed your guests the way we feed family."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "OMG you're going to LOVE our amazing food!!!"

### Rooted
- **We are:** specific about where the food comes from. We use the real dish names (Djouka, Zaamè, saka-saka) and explain them plainly the first time.
- **We are not:** a museum label or a travel brochure. We never call our food "exotic" or "ethnic".
- **Sounds like:** "Djouka, Malian peanut couscous: fonio steamed with roasted ground peanuts."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Experience the exotic flavors of a faraway land."

### Plain-spoken
- **We are:** clear and concrete. Short sentences, everyday words, what's actually on the plate.
- **We are not:** hype. No "best in Philly", "world-class", "elevated", "next level".
- **Sounds like:** "Lamb marinated in house spices and grilled over charcoal."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "An elevated, chef-driven lamb experience."

### Quietly confident
- **We are:** sure of the food and patient about the future. Twenty years of cooking speaks for itself.
- **We are not:** pushy. No countdowns, "limited time", or "book before it's too late".
- **Sounds like:** "Until the doors open, book us for your event."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Spots are filling FAST, book NOW!"

## What we always come back to

In this order:

1. **The African grill.** Live fire, charcoal and smoke, with West African spice. This is what we lead with and what sets us apart.
2. **Family and heritage.** A family business, rooted in our family's heritage. These are the dishes we grew up with.
3. **Whole, natural ingredients, cooked with care.** Real cuts, fresh produce, house spices, stews that simmer, pastry folded by hand.
4. **More than 20 years of experience.** Proof without bragging.
5. **Catering now, our own table in 2027.** We're bringing the food to you while our restaurant is renovated.

## Tone by situation

The voice stays the same; the tone shifts.

| Situation | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website, menus | Warm and specific | "Easy to pass around at parties and gatherings." |
| Booking and forms | Clear, helpful, brief | "Add roughly how many people you're feeding." |
| Confirmation | Grateful | "Thank you for thinking of us. We'll be in touch soon to plan your menu." |
| Something went wrong | Calm, blame-free, gives a next step | "We couldn't send that just now. Your details are below." |
| Social posts | Warm, a little playful, fire first | "Coals are lit. Lamb's on. Saturday's wedding is going to smell incredible." |
| 2027 opening news | Proud, unhurried | "Our own dining room, coming 2027." |

## Style rules

- **Headings:** sentence case. "Platters for the table", not "Platters For The Table".
- **Contractions:** yes. "We'll", "you're", "we've".
- **Lists:** no Oxford comma. "kabobs, lamb and steak".
- **Experience:** "more than 20 years" in sentences, "20+ years" in short labels. Not "over 20" or "twenty".
- **Phone:** (610) 256-9268. **Email:** fdgrillllc@gmail.com, lowercase.
- **Exclamation marks:** none on the website; one at most in a social post.
- **All caps:** never for emphasis.
- **Link and button text:** say what happens. "Get a catering quote", "Call (610) 256-9268". Never "Click here".

## Words we use and avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| grill, the African grill, live fire | barbecue, BBQ (in site copy) | "Grill" is our word; "#BBQ" is fine as a social hashtag |
| live fire, charcoal, smoke, char, coals | "flame-kissed", "smokin'" | Plain, sensory, true |
| wood-smoked, wood-fired | (only when true) | A claim foodies will check; use it only for food actually cooked over wood |
| whole ingredients, natural ingredients | all-natural, 100% natural, organic | "All-natural" and "organic" are regulated or litigated claims |
| kabob | kebab, kabab | One spelling everywhere |
| West African, Malian | African (alone, except in "the African grill"), ethnic, exotic | Be specific; never "other" the food |
| whole ingredients, house spices | authentic, artisanal, gourmet | Show it, don't label it |
| catering, your event | services, solutions | We cook for people, not "clients" |
| quote | pricing, packages | We price each event for its guest count |
| family-run, our family | family-owned and operated | Simpler |
| patté, Zaamè, Dégué | patty, Zaame, Degue | Keep the accents |

## Dish names

Write the dish name first, then a short plain-English description: **Djouka**, *Malian peanut couscous*. Keep accents: Patté, Zaamè, Dégué. Leaf stews are cassava leaf, saka-saka (potato greens) and fakoye (jute leaf).
