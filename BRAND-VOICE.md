# FD Grill brand voice

How FD Grill sounds, on the website, menus, flyers, social posts, emails and texts.

**Positioning:** Home of the African grill.
**Tagline:** From our family's table to yours.
**The bridge:** You already know this food.
**The promise:** The real thing, and everyone's invited.

The positioning says what we are, the tagline says how it feels, the bridge welcomes people who haven't tried West African food yet, and the promise says what never changes: we cook it for real, and we cook it for everyone. Together: "Home of the African grill. You already know this food. From our family's table to yours."

## Who we are, as a person

If FD Grill were a person, it would be the aunt or uncle running the grill at a family cookout. They've cooked these dishes for more than 20 years, they know every one by name, and they'd rather hand you a plate than give a speech. Proud of the food, generous with guests, happy when the yard is full, calm under a crowd.

## Who we're talking to

**Everyone in America who loves to eat and gather.** If you've stood around a backyard grill, waited on a pot of rice, or argued about whose greens are best, this food is for you. We don't write for one group; we write for the whole table.

Within that, four groups shape what we say:

1. **People who haven't had West African food yet.** Most of America. Many expect it to be unfamiliar, or not for them. It's closer than they think: meat charred over coals, rice cooked red with tomato, peanut stews, greens simmered low, hand pies and hibiscus drinks all have cousins in Southern, soul, Caribbean and Latin American cooking. Our job is to make the first plate feel like home.
2. **Food lovers who chase fire-cooked food.** They care how something was cooked and where the ingredients come from. Give them the fire, the spices, the cut, the hours on the stove.
3. **Hosts planning an event, big or small.** Birthdays, family reunions, weddings, graduations, office lunches, church events. They want food their guests will talk about, and someone reliable to cook it.
4. **People who already know these dishes.** West African families across the country. Their approval is our credibility: never water the food down or explain it in a way that talks past them.

We write for all of them as fellow food people. We never call anyone "foodies" in copy.

## Voice attributes

### Joyful and inviting
- **We are:** cookout energy. Celebratory, generous and a little playful. Food is a reason to get people together, and every line asks the reader in: pick a dish, bring your people, save a seat, tell us about your event.
- **We are not:** loud or pushy. No hype, no countdowns, no "limited time", no exclamation marks on the website. An invitation, never pressure.
- **Sounds like:** "Coals are lit. Bring your people." · "Pick what you love and we'll show you where to start."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "OMG the BEST party food EVER, book NOW!!!"

### Warm, like family
- **We are:** welcoming, personal, glad you're here. We talk about "your people", "your table", "your day".
- **We are not:** gushing, cute or over-familiar.
- **Sounds like:** "Feed your guests the way we feed family."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Hey foodies, you're going to LOVE this!"

### Fire-forward
- **We are:** sensory and precise about how food is cooked: live fire, charcoal, smoke, char, stews that simmer low and slow.
- **We are not:** gimmicky. No "flavor explosions", "mouthwatering", "to die for". We never claim a technique we don't use: say "wood-smoked" or "wood-fired" only when the food is cooked over wood.
- **Sounds like:** "Kabobs marinated in house spices, then charred over live charcoal until the edges crisp."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Our mouthwatering grill will blow your mind."

### Authentic
- **We are:** the real thing. Family recipes, cooked the way we cook them at home, nothing toned down and nothing dressed up. We use the real dish names (Djouka, Zaamè, saka-saka) and explain them plainly the first time. Welcoming new people never means changing the food; it means helping them find their way in.
- **We are not:** a museum label, a travel brochure or a watered-down version. Never "exotic" or "ethnic". We prove authenticity with specifics (whose recipe, how it's cooked, what's in it) rather than by repeating the word.
- **Sounds like:** "These are our family's recipes, cooked the way we cook them at home. Nothing toned down, nothing dressed up."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Experience authentic exotic flavors from a faraway land."

### Plain-spoken
- **We are:** clear and concrete. Short sentences, everyday words, what's actually on the plate. Twenty years of cooking speaks for itself.
- **We are not:** hype. No "best in Philly", "world-class", "elevated", "next level".
- **Sounds like:** "Lamb marinated in house spices and grilled over charcoal."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "An elevated, chef-driven lamb experience."

## What we always come back to

In this order:

1. **The African grill.** Live fire, charcoal and smoke, with West African spice. This is what we lead with.
2. **You already know this food.** Start from what people love, then introduce the dish (see below).
3. **The real thing.** Family recipes and more than 20 years of cooking them, from whole ingredients: real cuts, fresh produce, house spices, pastry folded by hand. Nothing toned down.
4. **Catering, big and small.** From a family dinner to a full reunion. We price each event for its guest count.
5. **A new chapter.** We already have our home at 6732 Elmwood Ave; it's being renovated into a full sit-down restaurant, opening 2027. Catering carries on as always. Tell it as a success story: we're growing, not starting out. Never say "20 years in the making", and never suggest we don't have a place yet. Invite people in: "Save me a seat."

## Start from what people already love

Lead with the connection, then introduce the dish.

- **Pattern:** "Love [familiar food]? Try [our dish]." Then describe the dish plainly.
- **Pairings we use:** backyard cookout → kabobs, collard greens → saka-saka, egg rolls → nem, empanadas or beef patties → patté, jambalaya or Spanish rice → zaamè, peanut butter → maafe, agua de jamaica or red drink → bissap (the same hibiscus flower).
- **Reach wide:** pick familiar foods from across American kitchens (Southern, soul, Cajun, Caribbean, Latin American, Chinese American takeout) so everyone finds a way in.
- **Tone:** warm and a little playful, never a lecture.
- **Never** talk down to the guest or to the food. No "don't be scared", "be brave", "give it a chance", "it's not weird", "exotic" or "acquired taste".
- Comparisons describe taste and texture; don't claim a dish *is* the familiar one.

## Tone by situation

The voice stays the same; the tone shifts.

| Situation | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website, menus | Warm and specific | "Easy to pass around at parties and gatherings." |
| Announcements, ticker | Joyful, short, one idea | "Now catering events, big and small." |
| First-timers | Inviting, familiar first | "Love collard greens? Try saka-saka." |
| Booking and forms | Clear, helpful, brief | "Add roughly how many people you're feeding." |
| Confirmation | Grateful | "Thank you for thinking of us. We'll be in touch soon to plan your menu." |
| Something went wrong | Calm, blame-free, gives a next step | "We couldn't send that just now. Your details are below." |
| Social posts | Joyful, fire first | "Coals are lit. Lamb's on. Saturday's reunion is going to smell incredible." |
| 2027 opening news | Proud, celebratory, inviting | "Announcing our dining room: sit-down dining comes to FD Grill in 2027. Save me a seat." |

## Style rules

- **Headings:** sentence case. "Platters for the table", not "Platters For The Table".
- **Contractions:** yes. "We'll", "you're", "we've".
- **Lists:** no Oxford comma. "kabobs, lamb and steak".
- **Experience:** "more than 20 years" in sentences, "20+ years" in short labels. Not "over 20" or "twenty".
- **Phone:** (610) 256-9268. **Email:** fdgrillllc@gmail.com, lowercase. **Address:** 6732 Elmwood Ave, Philadelphia, PA.
- **Exclamation marks:** none on the website; one at most in a social post.
- **All caps:** never for emphasis.
- **Link and button text:** say what happens. "Get a catering quote", "Call (610) 256-9268". Never "Click here".

## Dish names

Write the dish name first, then a short plain-English description: **Djouka**, *Malian peanut couscous*. Keep accents: Patté, Zaamè, Dégué. Leaf stews are cassava leaf, saka-saka (potato greens) and fakoye (jute leaf).
