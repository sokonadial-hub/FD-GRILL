# FD brand strategy: West African cooking, refined by fire

Working draft, October 2026. This document sets the strategy, positioning and image for FD as it grows from a catering business into a full-service restaurant opening in 2027. `BRAND-VOICE.md` covers day-to-day writing; this document sits above it.

---

## 1. The opportunity

West African food in Philadelphia is mostly presented as casual: counter service, strip-mall dining rooms, takeout. The cooking is often excellent; the setting and story rarely match it. That leaves room at the top of the market.

What's there today (from public listings and press, October 2026):

| Restaurant | What it is | Price signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Château Rouge**, 2108 South St | Cameroonian heritage with French technique; small, intimate BYOB; dinner only | $31–50 per person ([OpenTable](https://www.opentable.com/restaurant/profile/1458352)); ([Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/food/restaurants/chateau-rouge-restaurant)) |
| **Kilimandjaro**, University City | Senegalese, casual, long-standing; reopened 2024 | Casual ([Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/food/restaurants/youma-ba-kilimandjaro-west-african-restaurant-philadelphia-20240913.html), [The Infatuation](https://www.theinfatuation.com/philadelphia/reviews/kilimandjaro)) |
| **Amina**, Old City | Upscale, but contemporary Southern/American, not African | $$–$$$ (OpenTable listings) |
| **Louie Louie**, 3611 Walnut St | French-inspired American bistro; the benchmark for polish, service and room | $31–50 per person; Time Out 5/5 ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/philadelphia/restaurants/louie-louie-1)) |

**The gap:** no full-service West African restaurant in the city combines a designed dining room, live-fire cooking and the service polish of a place like Louie Louie. Château Rouge proves diners will pay $31–50 a head for refined African cooking; FD can go further on scale, fire and experience.

**Our advantage:** more than 20 years of family cooking, a menu that's already deep (Malian platters, leaf stews, grill), and a live-fire identity that food lovers actively seek out.

## 2. Positioning

**Positioning statement**

> For Philadelphians who seek out serious food, FD is the West African restaurant that cooks with live fire and family recipes, served with the care and polish of the city's best dining rooms. Unlike casual African spots, FD treats West African cooking as fine cuisine.

**Positioning line:** West African cooking, refined by fire.

**Tagline (kept):** From our family's table to yours.

**Why "refined by fire" instead of "home of barbecue":** "barbecue" pulls the brand toward paper plates and smokehouse platters. "Refined by fire" keeps everything food lovers love about smoke and live coals, and places it in a fine-dining frame. Use "barbecue" only in catering contexts where it helps people search, never on the restaurant.

## 3. Brand architecture

| | Now | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant (2027) | FD Grill | **FD** as the dining-room name, with a descriptor: *FD — West African Kitchen*. "Grill" signals casual. |
| Catering | FD Grill | **FD Catering** (or *FD Private Dining & Events*). Same family, premium event positioning. |
| Website | fdgrill.com | Keep fdgrill.com; consider adding fd-kitchen or similar later. Redirect, don't rebrand the domain twice. |

This is a recommendation, not a decision. A name change is the owners' call; if "FD Grill" stays, the rest of this strategy still works by leaning on design, menu and service.

---

## 4. Who we're for

1. **The serious diner (primary).** 28–55, eats out often, follows Philadelphia's restaurant press, books on Resy/OpenTable, will travel across the city for a meal. Has had suya or jollof, wants to go deeper.
2. **The celebrating host.** Birthdays, anniversaries, rehearsal dinners, corporate dinners. Wants a room and a meal that impress.
3. **The diaspora family.** Knows these dishes intimately and wants to see them honored, not watered down. Their approval is our credibility.
4. **Event clients (catering, now).** Hosts who want food their guests remember and a caterer that feels premium.

Write and design for #1; never alienate #3.

## 5. Brand pillars

| Pillar | What it means | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Heritage** | Family recipes, Malian and West African roots, more than 20 years of cooking | The family's story, dish names kept in original language, recipes credited to family |
| **Fire** | Live charcoal, smoke, char, slow-simmered pots | The hearth as the centerpiece of the room; grill visible from the dining room |
| **Craft** | Whole, natural ingredients; technique; plating | Sourcing named on the menu; refined plating; seasonal specials |
| **Hospitality** | Warm, polished service, like being hosted at home by people who care | Service standards, table-side explanation of dishes, bissap welcome |

## 6. Voice evolution

Keep the warmth. Add assurance and restraint.

| | Current | Refined |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | "The home of West African barbecue." | "West African cooking, refined by fire." |
| Story | "A family fire, 20 years in the making" | "Twenty years at the family hearth. Now, a table of our own." |
| Dish | "Steak in our house seasoning, seared hard over live charcoal." | "Ribeye, suya spice, charcoal-seared, onion-mustard jus." |
| Catering | "Let us cook for your people" | "Private dining and events, from our hearth to your table." |

**Rules:** fewer words; dish descriptions as ingredient lists, not sentences; no exclamation marks; never "authentic", "exotic" or "ethnic"; keep dish names in original language with a short English gloss.

## 7. Visual identity

### Palette (refined, from the family tablecloth and the fire)

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Charcoal | `#1A1714` | Backgrounds, menus, signage |
| Light | Bone | `#F3EDE3` | Paper, text on dark, tablecloth |
| Signature | Indigo | `#1F2A5C` | From the woven cloth; accents, uniforms, menu covers |
| Metal | Burnished ochre | `#A88433` | Fine rules, logo foil, wayfinding |
| Spark | Ember | `#C4622D` | Used sparingly: a single accent per page |

Less orange, more charcoal, bone and indigo. Orange as a spark, not a field.

### Typography (Adobe Fonts)

- **Display:** [Warbler Display](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/warbler) by David Jonathan Ross (DJR), or [Fiona](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/fiona) by Josh Ownby, for headlines, menu section titles and signage.
- **Text:** [Warbler Text](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/warbler) (same family as the display cut, for a unified system) or [Bogart](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/bogart) by Francesco Canovaro and Andrea Tartarelli (Zetafonts) for menus, body copy and the website.
- Both are Adobe Fonts. Web use needs an Adobe Fonts web project (kit) on the owners' Adobe account; print use needs the fonts activated in Creative Cloud.
- Retire the current condensed bold headline face: it's energetic and street-food in tone.

### Logo

The current round "FD GRILL" badge with a cartoon flame works for catering trays but not for a fine-dining room. Direction for a designer:
- A refined **FD monogram**, drawn with a single ember-line flame or hearth arch integrated into the letterforms.
- A wordmark set in the display face.
- One-color versions for foil stamping, embossing, signage and branded tableware.

### Photography

Stop: top-down plates on striped tablecloth under flat light, stock buffet trays, sausages and backyard grills.

Start:
- Low, warm, directional light; dark backgrounds; shallow depth of field.
- Plated dishes on handmade ceramics and dark wood.
- Fire close-ups: coals, smoke, hands turning skewers, steam from pots.
- The family at work, portraits with real character.
- The room: textures of indigo cloth, brass, wood, candlelight.

Budget for one professional shoot (food + family + fire) before the 2027 opening; it's the single most valuable brand investment after the room itself.

### Motif

Keep the woven indigo-and-ochre stripe as the signature texture, used with restraint: menu spines, napkin bands, a thin rule on the website. It's ours and it connects the restaurant to the family.

## 8. Experience, menu and price

- **Menu structure:** *To start* · *From the fire* · *From the pot* · *For the table* · *Sweets*. Shorter, seasonal, with two or three signature dishes that define FD (e.g., a whole-fish Djouka for two, charcoal lamb gigot, a tasting of leaf stews).
- **Price tier:** match the benchmarks: entrées roughly $28–45, $31–50+ per person, in line with Louie Louie and Château Rouge on OpenTable. A chef's tasting menu on weekends.
- **The bar: beer, and nothing else.** No cocktails, no wine. Treat that as a point of view, not a gap: beer is what you drink with grilled meat, smoke and spice. Keep it short and deliberate: a handful of cold beers, a mix of West African-style lagers and local Philadelphia brewers, each chosen to stand up to char and pepper, served properly cold in good glassware. Put one suggested beer next to each dish from the fire.
- **Without alcohol:** the house juices (bissap, ginger, baobab) are the other half of the drinks list, made in-house and presented with the same care as the beer.
- **Service rituals:** a welcome glass of bissap; dishes introduced with their family story; finish with Dégué or sweet tea.
- **Room:** warm low light, the hearth visible, indigo textiles, brass, wood. Music curated from West African and diaspora artists at conversation volume.

## 9. Path from catering to 2027

1. **Now: premium catering.** Reposition catering as *private dining & events*: plated or family-style service, branded presentation, the same refined photography and menus.
2. **2026: supper-club pop-ups.** Monthly ticketed dinners (Resy or Tock) in partner spaces. They build a guest list, generate press and test the menu and price point before opening.
3. **Press and partnerships.** Pitch the family story and the supper clubs to the Inquirer food desk, Philadelphia Magazine, Eater Philly and The Infatuation. Take part in community events such as Africatown Restaurant Week ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/food/restaurants/africatown-restaurant-week-philadelphia-20230412.html)).
4. **Opening waitlist.** Collect emails on the website now; give the list first access to opening reservations.
5. **Soft opening → opening.** Friends, family and the waitlist first; press after service is consistent.

## 10. Website implications

When the strategy is approved, the site should:
- Lead with "West African cooking, refined by fire", the room and the food, not "barbecue".
- Use the refined palette and the Adobe Fonts pairing.
- Replace stock photography with the professional shoot.
- Add: an opening waitlist, a supper-club/events page with ticket links, a private dining and catering inquiry that feels premium, and a press section once coverage lands.
- Keep the quote form, phone and email; restyle it for private dining.
